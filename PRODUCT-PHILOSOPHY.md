# Product Philosophy

## Core Truth

This is not primarily a geography quiz.

It is a playable cultural discovery engine.

The quiz exists to create curiosity and momentum. The real product begins after the guess.

## V1 Correction

The first playable direction became too poetic, too subtle, and too editorial.

For V1, the correct priority order is:

1. fun
2. discovery
3. poetry

The quiz should be fast, visual, and immediately understandable.

The reveal can still be atmospheric, but it must stay playful, bright, and obvious to interact with.

The cultural immersion happens after the guess. It is the reward, not the entrance exam.

Superseded note:

Any older references in this document to hidden scene hotspots, subtle environmental flirting, or world-first discovery navigation should be treated as archived exploration, not the active V1 product direction.

Active V1 interaction model:

1. fast, discriminative quiz
2. immediate country unlock
3. five obvious discovery choices: `TASTE`, `LISTEN`, `READ`, `SEE`, `CREATE`
4. one large media-first content pane
5. save and external-link actions

## Emotional Goal

The app should make people feel:

- curious
- surprised
- moved
- drawn toward a place they did not expect to care about

The magic moment is:

`Why do I suddenly want to know more about this country?`

That is the actual product outcome.

## Product Framing

Bad framing:

- educational trivia app
- geography game with extra facts
- country dashboard

Better framing:

- cultural tasting menu
- interactive world magazine
- emotional knowledge graph
- playful passport into other lives, sounds, tastes, and aesthetics

## Emotional Geography Principle

Country worlds are emotional geographies, not accurate dioramas.

They should be dreamlike spaces assembled from:

- cultural textures
- landscapes
- architecture
- sound
- everyday life
- the discoveries themselves

The goal is emotional truth, not cartographic accuracy.

## The Hook And The Reward

### Hook

The guess is fast, inviting, and low-friction.

- shape
- food
- photograph
- song snippet
- artist clue
- monument clue

### Reward

The reveal should feel rich and cinematic.

Not:

- a dense screen of cards
- a database feeling
- a statistics-heavy dashboard
- a click-through slideshow

Instead:

- a full-screen country world
- multiple subtle invitations embedded in the scene
- exploration driven by curiosity rather than forced order
- sound, image, motion, and atmosphere

## Tasting Menu Model

After a player guesses a country, the app should open a curated tasting world rather than a stack of cards.

The player should sense that five discoveries are present inside the world, but the order should be theirs to choose.

The tasting-menu idea still applies to the curation, but not as a forced sequence.

The five discoveries should be chosen as a coherent bundle, not assembled independently by category.

## Bundle Chemistry

The five discoveries in a visit should have internal chemistry.

They should feel like one curatorial mood or thread rather than five unrelated good picks.

Possible internal threads:

- `Sunday Morning`
- `After Dark`
- `By the Sea`
- `Made Here`
- `City and Stone`

These do not need to appear as explicit user-facing modes in V1, but the system should understand coherence.

## Curation Principle

Every country bundle should intentionally combine:

- `Iconic`
- `Everyday`
- `Contemporary`
- `Artistic`
- `Unexpected`

This protects the app from stereotype collapse.

Without this rule, every country risks becoming a flat list of clichés.

Each discovery should carry one primary recommendation.

Do not hedge inside a single discovery slot by trying to represent the entire country at once.

Depth should come from:

- the bundle
- revisits
- deeper country pools

## Curation Quality Bar

AI should not default to the most obvious, tourist-board, or first-page recommendation.

Technically correct but generic content is not enough.

Each recommendation should feel:

- genuinely curated
- worth sharing
- memorable
- emotionally sticky

The working test is:

`Would I tell a friend about this?`

If the answer is no, the recommendation is not strong enough yet.

## Not An Archive

The app is not trying to become an academic cultural archive.

It should surface cultural entries that people may genuinely want to:

- listen to
- cook
- read
- look up
- save
- visit

That means the bar is not only accuracy. The bar is lived interest.

## What Should Be Learned Indirectly

Some information matters, but should not dominate the first experience.

Examples:

- capital
- region
- subregion
- map placement

These are useful anchors, but they are not emotionally sticky enough to lead the product.

Best approach for V1:

- show them quietly in the reveal
- keep them available as context
- build dedicated geography modes later if the product earns them

## Experience Verbs

The V1 reveal should be framed through verbs rather than static content labels.

The core experience types are:

- `TASTE` -> food
- `LISTEN` -> music
- `READ` -> books
- `SEE` -> places, neighborhoods, landscapes, monuments, museums, or natural wonders
- `CREATE` -> artists, art, design, sculpture, photography, or other creative work

This framing is more active, inviting, and human than a card taxonomy.

`CREATE` stays as the fifth V1 discovery type.

Contemporary culture is not its own category. It is a curation principle woven across all five discovery types.

One discovery equals one primary recommendation.

## Passport Philosophy

The passport should not just collect countries.

It should collect discovery.

Examples:

- countries explored
- artists discovered
- books saved
- dishes bookmarked
- places you want to visit

Over time, the passport should reveal patterns in taste and curiosity.

Example:

`Your world is looking Mediterranean.`

That is a better long-term personalization direction than scorekeeping alone.

## AI's Proper Role

AI should not just generate random facts.

Its best role is:

- curating pathways
- spotting taste patterns
- connecting one discovery to another
- widening exploration without becoming generic

Example chain:

Lebanon -> Fairuz -> Arabic music -> Egypt -> Umm Kulthum -> Cairo -> Naguib Mahfouz

That is an emotional knowledge graph.

## Deep Country Pools

A country should not fundamentally own only five content items.

Long term, each country should have a deeper curated pool across:

- `TASTE`
- `LISTEN`
- `READ`
- `SEE`
- `CREATE`

A visit selects five discoveries that work well together as one coherent tasting menu.

A future return to the same country can surface a different, equally coherent bundle.

## People Matter

Countries should not feel abstract.

They should feel human.

That means the system should eventually include a `Meet` layer featuring people such as:

- artists
- filmmakers
- designers
- writers
- athletes
- scientists
- architects

This is stronger than reducing a country to symbols alone.

## Books Principle

Books do not need to be about the country.

They should be genuinely excellent books by authors from that country, chosen because they are worth reading, not because they can be forced into a national-summary role.

## Music Principle

Music should include artists people may genuinely want to keep listening to.

The app should not select music solely because it is historically important. It should balance significance with actual listening desire.

## Clue Principle

Mixed clues should teach something even before the reveal.

Even if the player guesses incorrectly, the round should leave behind a useful association, image, artist, dish, or place.

An incorrect answer should still produce:

- one remembered thing
- one new cultural association
- one reason to stay curious

## Visual Philosophy

The guess screen can be minimal and quiet.

The reveal should bloom.

The transition should feel like:

`the world opening up`

This makes the kaleidoscopic, rainbow, slightly psychedelic idea useful as a reveal language rather than a decorative gimmick.

## Interaction Philosophy

After the guess, the screen should transform into a dreamy, cartoony, illustrated cultural world inspired by that country.

It should not feel like:

- a literal map
- a tourism-board collage
- stereotypical national imagery pasted together
- a slideshow where the user clicks `Next` through five cards

It should feel like entering a small illustrated world.

Inside that world, the five discoveries should live as subtle interactive elements.

Examples:

- a spinning record or sound source -> `LISTEN`
- steam from a bakery or food detail -> `TASTE`
- a book placed somewhere in the scene -> `READ`
- a path, doorway, landscape, or architectural detail -> `SEE`
- a studio object, artwork, camera, sculpture, or creative fragment -> `CREATE`

The user should choose what they are curious about and in what order.

Curiosity should drive the exploration.

## Reactive World Principle

As discoveries are opened, the world should subtly react or evolve.

Examples:

- music keeps playing
- an object gains motion
- lighting shifts
- a detail glows or animates
- a passport note is quietly saved

This gives the world a sense of response without over-gamifying it.

## First 5 Minutes

The first five minutes should prove the product.

1. User lands on a visually inviting start screen.
2. User gets one clue with almost no friction.
3. User guesses.
4. The country opens in a rich reveal.
5. The user notices multiple invitations inside the world and follows one.
6. The app makes the user want one more country.

If that works, the rest of the product can scale.

## Success Test

After exploring a country, the user should feel curious enough to:

- listen to something
- cook something
- read something
- save something
- want to visit

If the user simply feels like they learned five facts, the product is failing its core promise.
