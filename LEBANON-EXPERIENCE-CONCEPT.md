# Lebanon Experience Concept

## Purpose

This document defines Lebanon as the first real product test.

It is written as a narrated first-time user experience, not as a generic feature description. The goal is to pressure-test:

- the emotional flow
- the interaction model
- the curation quality
- the world design logic

If Lebanon works, it becomes the template for how the product should feel. If it does not, the interaction model should change before implementation.

## V1 Correction

The earlier Lebanon direction became too quiet, too poetic, and too hidden in its interaction design.

The corrected Lebanon target is:

- fast quiz
- bright playful scene
- obvious discovery objects
- strong reward feeling after a correct answer

Fun first. Discovery second. Poetry third.

Superseded note:

This document originally explored a scene-first Lebanon world with environmental hotspots. That is no longer the active prototype direction.

The implemented Lebanon V1 now uses:

1. a fast validated quiz question
2. a simple `Welcome to Lebanon` reveal
3. five obvious discovery category buttons
4. one large media-first content pane with real cultural assets

## North Star

After five minutes in Lebanon, the user should not feel:

- informed
- completed
- finished with the content

They should feel:

- drawn in
- slightly enchanted
- newly curious
- tempted to keep listening, reading, or exploring

The target response is:

`Wait, Lebanon feels amazing in this app. What else is here?`

## World Principle

Lebanon should be built as an emotional geography, not an accurate diorama.

The world should be geographically impossible but emotionally true:

- a balcony that feels Beirut-adjacent
- the Mediterranean impossibly close below
- mountains rising too close behind buildings
- a bakery half-embedded in the architecture
- a staircase disappearing into rock
- laundry moving in the sea wind
- creative objects subtly becoming part of the environment

This is the right visual logic for the first visit.

## Curation Audit First

Before defining the interaction, the bundle itself has to earn the right to exist.

The current working Lebanon bundle is:

- `TASTE` -> Man'oushe
- `LISTEN` -> Fairuz and Mashrou' Leila
- `READ` -> Amin Maalouf, starting with `The Rock of Tanios`
- `SEE` -> Jeita Grotto
- `CREATE` -> Saloua Raouda Choucair

### Does It Balance The Five Principles?

#### Iconic

- The cedar clue and the Lebanon frame itself can carry some of the iconic recognition.
- The bundle does not need the `LISTEN` slot to carry all the iconic burden by itself.

#### Everyday

- Man'oushe gives it an everyday, lived-in anchor.

#### Contemporary

- Mashrou' Leila introduces a current and charged cultural energy.

#### Artistic

- Saloua Raouda Choucair gives the bundle real formal artistic depth.

#### Unexpected

- Jeita Grotto is strong here because it takes the player somewhere luminous and less overused than the obvious urban postcard.

### Does It Pass The "Would I Tell A Friend About This?" Test?

#### TASTE: Man'oushe

Yes.

This is strong because it feels immediate, specific, and everyday. It creates appetite and atmosphere fast.

#### LISTEN: Fairuz + Mashrou' Leila

No, not in this form.

The content is good, but the slot is doing too much. One discovery should equal one primary recommendation.

This slot currently hedges between iconic and contemporary instead of committing to a mood.

#### READ: `The Rock of Tanios`

Maybe.

This is the weakest current choice, not because it is bad, but because it may not create enough immediate reading desire for a first-time user.

It is elegant and serious, but it may feel more dutiful than magnetic.

### READ Revision

For the first Lebanon experience, the stronger choice is:

- `An Unnecessary Woman` by Rabih Alameddine

Why:

- it is more immediately tell-a-friend-able
- it feels literary without feeling assigned
- it carries Beirut energy more directly
- it makes the `READ` slot feel like an actual recommendation, not a respectable placeholder

This does not mean Amin Maalouf disappears from the wider repository. It means the first encounter should optimize for desire.

#### SEE: Jeita Grotto

Yes.

It is visually tempting, spatially useful, and helps the bundle escape pure city-symbol logic.

#### CREATE: Saloua Raouda Choucair

Yes.

This is an excellent `CREATE` pick because it raises the taste level of the whole experience.

### Bundle Chemistry

For the first Lebanon visit, the stronger curatorial thread is:

`Sunday Morning By The Sea, With A City Still Inside It`

This supports:

- warm breakfast
- intimate but contemporary sound
- inward reading
- stone and water depth
- quiet formal artistic intelligence

### LISTEN Revision

For the first Lebanon visit, the music slot should commit to:

- `Mashrou' Leila`

Why:

- it gives the bundle a clearer present-tense mood
- it prevents the discovery from becoming a mini-survey
- it makes the first visit feel more curated and less dutiful

Fairuz should remain in the deeper Lebanon pool and surface through:

- another clue
- another Lebanon visit
- a later cultural pathway

### Final Lebanon First-Visit Bundle

The refined Lebanon first-visit bundle should be:

- `TASTE` -> Man'oushe
- `LISTEN` -> Mashrou' Leila
- `READ` -> Rabih Alameddine, `An Unnecessary Woman`
- `SEE` -> Jeita Grotto
- `CREATE` -> Saloua Raouda Choucair

This bundle feels coherent, specific, and worth retelling.

## The First-Time User Experience

What follows is the Lebanon experience as a narrated playthrough.

## 0. Entry State

The user is on the quiz screen.

The quiz UI is quiet and clean. The screen is mostly air and one clue. The mood is inviting, not busy.

### Opening Mixed Clue

The clue is text-led with a contemporary sound suggestion embedded in it:

`Warm flatbread dusted with za'atar. A band that made Arab indie feel sharper, riskier, and more alive. A small Mediterranean country with a cedar on its flag. Which country is it?`

### Answer Options

- Lebanon
- Syria
- Jordan
- Greece

This is a good Lebanon opening clue because:

- it teaches something before the answer
- it introduces both a sound and a food association
- it is not just flag trivia
- it leans toward the first-visit bundle rather than generic country facts
- it is fair without feeling flat

## 1. Wrong-Answer Behavior

If the user chooses the wrong answer, the app does not punish them with a dead end.

### Example

The user taps `Syria`.

The app responds:

`Close. This was Lebanon. Man'oushe is one of the foods that makes everyday Lebanon feel immediate, and Mashrou' Leila became a defining sound for a more contemporary Arab indie scene.`

Then the world still opens, but in a softer state.

### Softer State Means

- the scene still appears
- only two invitations feel immediately active
- passport credit is partial
- the user can still experience Lebanon

This keeps the round generous while still preserving the value of a correct guess.

### Partial Passport Save On Wrong Answer

Saved:

- `country glimpsed: Lebanon`

Not yet saved:

- full country exploration
- discovery count
- completion state

## 2. Correct Reveal Transition

If the user chooses `Lebanon`, the answer state should feel more like a transformation than a result screen.

### Transition Sequence

1. The answer options soften and blur.
2. A low warm chord and distant city-sea ambience begins.
3. The background color blooms from pale paper tones into warmer coral, dusk-blue, amber, and sea green.
4. The clue text breaks into drifting fragments like paper caught in air.
5. The scene deepens instead of cutting.

The user should feel:

`I am entering somewhere`

not:

`I advanced to the next panel`

## 3. What The Lebanon World Looks Like

The Lebanon world is a single illustrated dream-hybrid suspended between city, sea, bakery warmth, and stone.

It is not a literal map.

It is not a travel poster.

It is not a cedar-tree mood board.

It is a small emotional geography.

### Composition

#### Foreground

- a tiled balcony edge that also feels almost like a cliff
- a small round metal table
- an open book with one page lifting in the breeze
- a folded paper wrapping around warm man'oushe
- a tiny radio or record speaker with a gold dial

#### Midground

- layered Beirut-adjacent rooftops
- hanging fabric catching light
- a bakery glow half-embedded in the architecture
- a narrow stairway disappearing between buildings and then into rock
- a geometric sculpture form hanging near a shaded interior doorway

#### Background

- Mediterranean water impossibly close below the architecture
- hazy mountain silhouette rising too near behind the buildings
- light that feels like late morning leaning toward afternoon

### Visual Style

- dreamy
- cartoony
- painterly
- textured like printed paper and colored pencil
- gently surreal

### Tone

The world feels:

- intimate
- warm
- faintly musical
- a little wind-touched
- full of small signals that life is happening nearby
- physically impossible in a way that feels right

## 4. How The World Flirts With The User

There are no obvious labeled buttons floating over the illustration.

Instead, the world tempts interaction through visual oddity and sensory suggestion.

### LISTEN Temptation

The small radio is not pulsing like a button.

Instead:

- a thread of sound seems to drift from it
- the gold dial catches light in a rhythmic way
- nearby fabric moves very slightly in time

The user thinks:

`Wait, is that where the music is coming from?`

### TASTE Temptation

The man'oushe wrapper is slightly open.

Instead of a static image:

- steam curls upward
- the steam briefly twists into an almost calligraphic shape before thinning out
- za'atar crumbs catch the light

The user thinks:

`That looks warm. What is that?`

### READ Temptation

The book is open on the table but not highlighted.

Instead:

- one page lifts, nearly turns, then falls back
- a margin note flashes into visibility when the light passes across it

The user thinks:

`There is something there. What book is that?`

### SEE Temptation

The stairway and far opening into the scene are not labeled.

Instead:

- a faint cool glow appears deep in the right side of the world
- a shimmer of reflected water seems to come from somewhere impossible inland

The user thinks:

`Where does that go?`

### CREATE Temptation

The sculptural object near the doorway does something slightly wrong in the best way.

Instead:

- one geometric piece shifts a fraction even though there is no visible hand
- its shadow falls in a pattern that feels too deliberate to ignore

The user thinks:

`What is that object?`

This is the correct flirting behavior:

- no obvious UI labels
- no generic hotspot halos
- enough movement, sound, or light to trigger curiosity

## 5. The Exact Discovery Content

These are the exact V1 Lebanon discoveries and the copy tone they should use.

## TASTE

### Recommendation

`Man'oushe`

### On Open Copy

`Still warm from the oven, brushed with olive oil and covered with za'atar, man'oushe is the kind of breakfast that tells you a place through habit rather than ceremony. You eat it fast, standing up, carrying it into the day, or tearing it apart with someone.`

### Why This Works

- everyday, not formal
- sensory
- tells a friend something real
- instantly appetite-making

## LISTEN

### Recommendation

`Mashrou' Leila`

### On Open Copy

`Sharp, literate, restless, and emotionally exposed, Mashrou' Leila opened up a contemporary sound that many people encountered as an entirely different image of Arab music: urban, intimate, confrontational, and unmistakably alive.`

### Why This Works

- musically desirable, not merely important
- gives the bundle present-tense edge
- commits to one recommendation instead of hedging

## READ

### Recommendation

`Rabih Alameddine — An Unnecessary Woman`

### On Open Copy

`A brilliant, solitary woman in Beirut, a lifetime of reading, and a city felt through intellect, memory, and mood. This is the kind of recommendation that makes Lebanon feel literary without turning literature into homework.`

### Why This Works

- stronger immediate desire than the previous pick
- feels like an actual recommendation
- still has real literary weight

## SEE

### Recommendation

`Jeita Grotto`

### On Open Copy

`Cathedral-sized chambers, underground water, and stone that feels carved by time into something almost theatrical. Jeita widens Lebanon beyond city life and history into something luminous, geologic, and slightly unreal.`

### Why This Works

- visually magnetic
- spatially useful in the scene
- unexpected enough to feel discovered

## CREATE

### Recommendation

`Saloua Raouda Choucair`

### On Open Copy

`A pioneer of Arab modernism whose work turns geometry into rhythm. Her sculpture feels architectural, musical, and deeply self-possessed, which makes her a perfect creative key into Lebanon's sharper artistic side.`

### Why This Works

- high taste
- not obvious
- artistic without becoming obscure-for-obscurity's-sake

## 6. What Happens When Each Discovery Opens

The user stays inside the world. The content should appear as an expansion of the scene, not as a replacement.

## If The User Opens LISTEN

### Interaction

- the user taps or hovers near the radio
- the sound thread expands
- a listening panel unfolds beside it like a paper note or lyric sleeve

### World Reaction

- music begins softly
- hanging fabric sways more visibly
- the palette sharpens slightly, with deeper contrast through the city colors

### Passport Save

Saved:

- `country explored: Lebanon`
- `LISTEN discovered`
- `artist discovered: Mashrou' Leila`

Optional save affordances appear quietly for:

- `save artist`
- `listen later`

## If The User Opens TASTE

### Interaction

- the user taps the steam or bread wrapper
- the paper opens a little further
- the food note rises from the table as if lifted by heat

### World Reaction

- steam becomes more visible
- herb tones deepen
- nearby surfaces feel warmer in color

### Passport Save

Saved:

- `TASTE discovered`
- `dish discovered: Man'oushe`

Optional quiet affordance:

- `save recipe`

## If The User Opens READ

### Interaction

- the user taps the lifting page
- the page finally turns
- a short literary note appears inside the open spread

### World Reaction

- wind sound becomes more audible for a second
- the city ambience softens
- a handwritten underline appears in the margin

### Passport Save

Saved:

- `READ discovered`
- `book discovered: An Unnecessary Woman`
- `author discovered: Rabih Alameddine`

Optional quiet affordance:

- `save book`

## If The User Opens SEE

### Interaction

- the user taps the glowing depth cue near the stairway / distant opening
- the scene subtly deepens as if the world is leaning open
- a location window appears without fully leaving the dream-city scene

### World Reaction

- cool reflected light enters the frame
- water shimmer appears across the balcony floor
- the horizon gains more depth

### Passport Save

Saved:

- `SEE discovered`
- `place discovered: Jeita Grotto`
- `want to visit` prompt can appear later, not immediately

## If The User Opens CREATE

### Interaction

- the user taps the shifting sculptural form
- it expands into a cleaner geometric arrangement
- a visual note about Choucair appears like a gallery label that belongs inside the world

### World Reaction

- geometric shadows ripple
- the scene gains a more noticeable formal structure
- color blocks become slightly crisper

### Passport Save

Saved:

- `CREATE discovered`
- `artist discovered: Saloua Raouda Choucair`

Optional quiet affordance:

- `save artist`

## 7. What Happens After 1 Discovery

After one discovery, the user should feel:

- rewarded
- not finished
- aware that the world is still alive

### System Behavior

- one passport glint appears quietly
- a tiny line of copy can surface:
  - `Lebanon is opening up.`
- one or two unopened elements become a little easier to notice

This is not gamified completion language. It is invitation.

## 8. What Happens After 3 Discoveries

At three discoveries, the world should feel noticeably richer.

### System Behavior

- more ambient sound layers are active
- the scene feels fuller
- the user receives a quiet pattern insight

Example:

`You found sound, food, and books first. Your Lebanon is becoming intimate.`

### Passport Save State

Saved:

- `Lebanon explored`
- 3 discovery types
- associated items

At this point, the user should be strongly tempted either to complete the world or to save something and come back.

## 9. What Happens After All 5 Discoveries

This should not feel like "100% complete."

It should feel like:

`I saw the shape of this country, and now I want more.`

### System Behavior

- the full ambient world comes alive at once
- music, light, movement, and geometry all sit together
- the passport records a full Lebanon exploration

### Quiet Completion Copy

`You found one way into Lebanon.`

This is better than:

- `Completed`
- `Mastered`
- `Done`

Because the product is about pathways, not closure.

### Passport Save On Full Exploration

Saved:

- `country explored: Lebanon`
- all 5 discovery types
- discovered items:
  - Man'oushe
  - Mashrou' Leila
  - An Unnecessary Woman
  - Jeita Grotto
  - Saloua Raouda Choucair

Optional future save states:

- artists saved
- book saved
- recipe saved
- places to visit

## 10. Staying In Lebanon Or Traveling Somewhere New

The exit choice should not feel like a hard menu.

After some exploration, two subtle directions should become available:

### Stay In Lebanon

This means:

- reopen discoveries
- save items
- linger in the scene
- optionally unlock a deeper second-layer detail later

Possible copy:

`Stay a little longer`

### Travel Somewhere New

This means:

- return to the world quiz
- carry the emotional residue of Lebanon into the next country

Possible copy:

`Travel onward`

Both options should feel poetic and light, not system-heavy.

## 11. First Five Minutes Storyboard

### Minute 0 to 1

The user sees the clue, guesses Lebanon, and the scene opens.

They notice the radio first because the sound drift is the clearest invitation.

They open `LISTEN`.

Mashrou' Leila begins softly.

Now the scene feels inhabited.

### Minute 1 to 2

The user notices the steam lifting from the bread.

They open `TASTE`.

Now the world feels warmer and more tactile.

They are no longer in a quiz. They are in a mood.

### Minute 2 to 3

The page on the table lifts.

They open `READ`.

The scene quiets just enough for the literary recommendation to land.

Now Lebanon feels thoughtful, not just sensorial.

### Minute 3 to 4

They notice the impossible watery glow deeper in the scene.

They open `SEE`.

Jeita Grotto gives the country a spatial surprise.

Now the player understands Lebanon is not being reduced to one urban image.

### Minute 4 to 5

They finally notice the geometric form near the doorway shifting against the wall.

They open `CREATE`.

Choucair reframes the whole world with a sharper artistic intelligence.

By now the user should feel:

- I want to listen to Mashrou' Leila for real
- I would try that breakfast
- that book sounds worth saving
- I did not expect the cave or the sculpture

That is the correct success state.

## Final Product Test

Lebanon is successful if a first-time user leaves the experience wanting at least one of these:

- to press play
- to save the book
- to look up the artist
- to make the food
- to visit the place

If they merely learned that Lebanon has music, food, books, caves, and art, then the product has failed.
